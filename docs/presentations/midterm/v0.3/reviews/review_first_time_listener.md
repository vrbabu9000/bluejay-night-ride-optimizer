# Review: first-time listener (midterm v0.3)

Reviewer stance: a graduate student in an applied-math course who has never seen the project. I read all 32 pages of `BlueJay_Midterm_v0.3_preview.pdf` and only the spoken blocks and spoken transitions of `speaking_script.md`. Review date: 22 Oct 2026.

## Overall verdict

I could follow this talk. The outcome tree on slide 2 gives me a backbone, the data-investigation slides (6–10) each make one point I can repeat afterwards, and the results (18–20) return to that tree in a way that pays off. I would get lost in four places. First, the word "booking" ends up meaning two different things: a Night Ride request and a Lyft order. Second, the 6 pm ride count appears as three different numbers on slides 4–6, and the speaker says a number (168) that never appears on screen. Third, slide 12 tries to teach a loop, a MILP, a points system and a caveat about optimality all in 75 seconds. Fourth, the talk never directly answers the question it asks on slide 2. The pacing (about 2,290 spoken words, roughly 114 words per minute on average) is realistic. The time is unevenly spent, though: the densest slides (2, 12, 15) are rushed, and the densest chart (19) gets the slowest delivery.

## Pacing note

The script has about 2,290 spoken words. Per slide, it ranges from ~93 wpm (slide 19) to ~141 wpm (slide 2) and ~134 wpm (slide 12). The total fits in 20 minutes, but the time is in the wrong places: the vocabulary slide (2) and the method slide (12) are the fastest, while the densest chart (19) is the slowest. Give slides 2 and 12 more time, taken from 17, 19 and 22.

## What works well

- The outcome tree on slide 2 is an excellent backbone, and slides 18–19 explicitly return to its two branches.
- The two-table pairing example on slide 7 makes "the data cannot identify this" concrete in five seconds.
- The two fractions on slide 10 are the clearest "same count, different rate" demonstration I have seen in a student talk.
- Slide 14 separates the prediction (hollow dot, "a prediction only") from realized events, and states plainly that the van counterfactual is never observed.
- The verification-versus-validation split (slide 17) and the NOT IMPLEMENTED tag on slide 21 are honest and easy to read.
- Rule names are plain English throughout the main talk. Codes (R0, L5, D-numbers) appear only in footers and in backup B1.
- The dominance mini-picture on slide 20 teaches the concept before applying it.
- Each backup links back to its parent slide, which will make Q&A smooth.
