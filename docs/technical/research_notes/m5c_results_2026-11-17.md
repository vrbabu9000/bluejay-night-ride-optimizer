## How to read it
- **C-F costs riders.** At high load the interval excludes 0: C-F leaves about 2 pp more requests unreached within 20 min than C (36.2% vs 34.2%). C-F was worse on 17 of 29 nights, better on 7, tied on 5. Whether the cost exceeds the 2-pp margin is not settled at 29 nights. Base load points the same way (+1.0 pp; worse on 17 of 30 nights).
- **The gate miss is narrow and is not reinterpreted.** The median was 15.2 min against a 15-min threshold. The probe on r00–r03 had already shown the gate was borderline, and the threshold was deliberately left unchanged then. It stays a fail.
- **The free drop-off order earns its cost.** This is the first measurement of what the exact reordering in C (D-52 amendment) buys in rider outcomes, rather than objective points: about 1–2 pp of requests reached within 20 min.
- **C-F still clearly beats greedy** (descriptive, no test): high load −9.51 pp [−11.97, −7.10]; base −5.31 pp [−7.29, −3.39]. So the M5b answer to the instructor's question does not depend on exact reordering; most of C's advantage comes from re-planning all open work together.
- **Secondary measures, C-F − C (mean per night, exploratory):** P90 passenger wait +0.05 min (high) / +0.53 min (base); P90 van ride −0.01 / −0.09 min; cancellations +1.1 / +0.5 per night.

## What this does not show
- Full evenings (M6/M7).
- Whether some other speed-up (a time cap per re-plan, a smaller K at high load) would pass. Any such variant would need its own frozen test.
