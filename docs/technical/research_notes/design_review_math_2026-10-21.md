# Independent review: mathematical consistency of the design (21 Oct 2026)

**In plain words.** The core of the model holds up: the wait penalty is computed correctly and passes the TransLoc check, the units line up, and the scenario generator stores the unscaled travel time, as the spec says it should. Five things need fixing before anyone writes simulator code or freezes the pre-registration:
1. `model_design.md` applies the travel multiplier twice.
2. The claim that "a request stays pending only when no route can take it" is false once the candidate cap applies, and the Lyft rules then read that artificial pending status.
3. The documents disagree on whether the ride guardrail is hard or elastic, and the "always feasible" route can break a hard guardrail.
4. M, Ψ and the HiGHS gap have no numbers, and the default relative gap is meaningless when the objective contains M.
5. The pre-registration's decision rule treats "the CI includes 0" as "not worse", and its P90 wait is survivor-biased.

The financial terms listed in F-20 can be removed without touching the dispatcher objective.

**Scope and method.** I read the whole of `model_design.md` (v0.1), `scenario_spec.md` §2–§7 (v0.3), the whole of `preregistration_v2.md`, `decision_log.md` (D-29 and everything from "Added on 21 Oct 2026" onward), and `research_notes/math_literature.md` §2–§5 (background only). I read `src/scenario/generator_v2.py` and checked one frozen L5 file (`data/scenarios/v2/L5/r00`) against the travel matrix, without changing either. I ran no simulations and edited no other file. Line numbers refer to the files as they were on 21 Oct 2026.

**Classes used in the table:**
- **Resolved:** checked and correct.
- **Transparent:** a base assumption or wording point; the fix is to state it.
- **Deferred:** a sensitivity case for later.
- **Blocker:** must be settled before implementation, or before the pre-registration is frozen. The table says which.

## 1. Units, checked (F-01 to F-06)

The objective is consistent once three things are true:
- times are converted to minutes before φ is applied, as the generator already does;
- every leg is computed as round(matrix seconds × m) in whole seconds;
- T_r is the scaled direct drive with no second multiplier.

The generator's L1 cost (`_best_van`, L184–195) is the D-29 objective exactly:

q·φ(deadhead) + 0.5·q·(30 s + drive)/60 + 0.1·km

The only document errors are in `model_design.md`:
- the m² on L115 and L230;
- the β description on L160.

## 2. Pending price under joint competition (F-08, F-09)

**Counterexample.** One van, cap |S_k| ≤ 1 (or a pair whose joint route breaks the guardrail or capacity), and two open single-rider requests A (waited 12 min) and B (waited 1 min). The candidate routes are k⁰ = {}, k_A = {A} and k_B = {B}. Both A and B have a feasible candidate route, yet Σ_k y_k = 1 forces one of them to stay pending. The claims "any feasible assignment beats leaving r pending" and "a request stays pending only when no candidate route can take it" are therefore both false. Assigning B instead of A is a swap, not an improvement: the M terms cancel.

**Correct statement.** Let M exceed the largest increase in route cost caused by adding one request to any candidate. Then each epoch solution (i) covers the largest possible number of open bookings (or passengers, if M is weighted by q_r) given the enumerated candidates and one route per van, and (ii) among such covers, minimises the remaining cost. A request stays pending either because no enumerated candidate contains it, or because every candidate containing it competes for a van needed to cover more requests. Pending status is therefore partly an artifact of the candidate restriction. The Lyft rules must not read it as "no van can serve this rider" (F-09).

## 3. Proposed decision wording for prereg §4 (F-15)

Lower is better for both primaries. Let d = A − B be the paired per-night difference, with a 95% bootstrap CI [lo, hi]. The margins are δ_P90 = 1 min and δ_share = 2 pp (the stated minimum meaningful differences); δ_L for Lyft bookings per night is for the team to set.
- **Non-inferior:** hi < +δ on each primary.
- **Superior:** hi < 0 (meaningfully superior: hi < −δ).
- **Fewer Lyft trips:** hi(Lyft_A − Lyft_B) < 0.
- **Equal Lyft use:** −δ_L < lo and hi < δ_L (TOST equivalence).
- **A dominates B** if A is non-inferior on both primaries and uses fewer Lyft trips, or A is superior on at least one primary, non-inferior on the other, and has equal Lyft use.

Otherwise, report both rules on the frontier. **A CI that contains 0 is reported as "inconclusive", never as "not worse".**
