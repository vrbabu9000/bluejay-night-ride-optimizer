No crash. 754 passed every check; 26 failed one check, and all 26 match the M7 §3c pattern (run record). All 30 nights are used; the strict version uses 19.

**What kind of result:** a **sensitivity analysis, not a test**. The plan was written after the M7 results were seen. The settings are plausible ranges, not estimates. Nothing here is a recommendation.

## 1. Headline
- **The two predicted-wait rules hold up.** R2(15) and R3(2) reach clearly more riders than vans-only in **every** setting, with all 95% upper bounds below 0, in the main and in the strict version.
- **The elapsed-wait rules do not.** R1(10) and R1(15) lose their effect when riders are less patient, when Lyft is slower or when fewer riders accept.
- **The size of the gain depends most on rider patience.** For R3(2) it is −8.2 points at base, −4.6 with impatient riders and −11.0 with patient ones.
- **Reading (b) holds in all 7 settings.** The point estimate of gain per booking for R2(15) and R3(2) exceeds R1(10)'s everywhere.

## 5. Why riders transferred to Lyft still go unreached
Mean share of transferred riders who cancel before the Lyft arrives (%, descriptive):

| Setting | R1(10) | R1(15) | R2(15) | R3(2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 49 | 41 | 32 | 30 |
| patience × 2/3 | 66 | 74 | 49 | 40 |
| patience × 3/2 | 28 | 34 | 18 | 16 |
| Lyft delay × 2/3 | 36 | 36 | 23 | 20 |
| Lyft delay × 3/2 | 60 | 56 | 46 | 41 |
| acceptance 80% | 47 | 37 | 34 | 30 |
| acceptance 60% | 45 | 50 | 29 | 30 |

- **This is the mechanism behind §2.** An elapsed-wait rule transfers a rider who has already waited 10 or 15 minutes, so a slower Lyft or a less patient rider means most transfers arrive too late. The predicted-wait rules transfer earlier.
- **Bookings are unchanged by Lyft delay** (same bookings per 100 as base). The rules decide before the delay is known, and R3(2) uses its built-in 8-min assumption (plan §2). Only what happens after booking changes.

## 6. Compute
- C's mean wall time per evening rises with patience, because more riders wait in the queue: R0 averages 8.8 min (impatient), 23.8 min (base) and 58.7 min (patient). With transfers it is 3–38 min.
- **Interruptions:** the laptop slept (lid closed, 25–26 Nov overnight) and hibernated once on low battery (26 Nov 14:23–15:48). The runs resumed in place. The solver's budget is a node limit, and its time cap did not count the sleep (two runs spanning the hibernation reported 50 and 17 min of run time). So the interruptions could not change any result (D-46).

## 7. What this does and does not show
- **Shows:** the qualitative M7 five-van finding (predicted-wait rules reach clearly more riders than vans-only, and more per booking than R1(10)) survives each assumption being moved one at a time across a plausible range.
- **Does not show:** calibrated values; joint changes; busy, far-trip or high-load nights; six vans; real Lyft behaviour; any recommendation (plan §5).
- **For the report:** the level of the gain (5–11 points for R3(2)) is assumption-dependent; the ranking R3(2) ≥ R2(15) > R1 rules is not.
