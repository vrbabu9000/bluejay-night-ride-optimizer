## 2. The one failed check (C, r02): diagnosed, explained
- **What failed:** the check "no unexplained van ride over the guardrail (excesses only from no-show waits, D-47/D-52)". It concerned one rider, R046 (party 1, van V3), whose ride was 196 s over the guardrail. The check allows 150 s for a no-show wait, plus 30 s for one drop dwell, plus a 13-s matrix tolerance: 193 s in total.
- **What happened:**
  1. At 18:00 + 3,844 s, V3 had three parties aboard (R041 party 3, R046, R063) and reached R066's curb. R066 was absent, so the van waited the 180-s curb timer, and R066 became a van no-show at 4,024 s.
  2. Before the wait, the plan delivered R046 at 4,222 s, within the guardrail.
  3. After the wait, **no drop order keeps both R041 and R046 within the guardrail**. Recomputed from the travel matrix:
     - R041 first: R041 is 0 s over and R046 196 s over, a total of 196 passenger-seconds;
     - R046 first: R046 is 24 s over and R041 (3 passengers) 99 s over each, a total of 321 passenger-seconds.
  4. The dispatcher's penalty (Ψ, per passenger-second over the guardrail, D-47) chose the smaller total, as designed.
- **Why the check flagged it:** the check assumes each rider's excess is at most one no-show wait plus one drop dwell. Here the dispatcher shifted an unavoidable excess between riders to minimise the total, so one rider carries more than the per-rider allowance. This is a **limit of the check's attribution**, not a dispatch defect. Every epoch's plan audit passed.
- **What is done about it:**
  - The fingerprinted check code (`l5_pilot.py`) is **not** changed (it would change the code fingerprint mid-study).
  - The M7 plan (§3c, frozen before any M7 run) keeps a night with this specific, demonstrated pattern, and reports the analysis with all failed-check nights excluded as a sensitivity.
  - M6 is descriptive, so r02 stays in the tables above, labelled.
