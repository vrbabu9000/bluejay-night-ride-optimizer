## How to read it
- **The answer to the instructor's question** (proposal feedback, point 2): on the first-hour peak of derived five-van nights, re-optimizing all open work with C reaches about 6 pp more requests within 20 min than myopic insertion that never revisits.
- **The gap grows with load per van:**
  - about 4 pp at 6 vans;
  - about 6 pp at 5 vans;
  - about 12 pp at high load.

  This is the "under what conditions" part. The cells are not compared formally (D-49: patterns are not paired with each other).
- **The re-plan interval (cell 4) barely matters.** This is **not** evidence that slow re-planning is fine:
  - C also re-plans at every event (request, cancellation, no-show);
  - the timer adds only extra re-plans with no new information;
  - on three base nights, the event-triggered epochs number 235–237 at every interval, while timer-only epochs fall from 276 (60 s) to 54 (300 s).

  So cell 4 measures extra timer re-plans, not how quickly C reacts to news.
- **Why it might hold, and what would weaken it:**
  - G is a fair but simple baseline: insertion that never reassigns, with no K cap.
  - The 30 nights are derived, not observed.
  - Patience, presence and the Lyft delay are ASSUMED placeholders, so the levels (22% vs 29%) are not Hopkins facts. The paired difference is the claim.

## What this does not show
- Whole-evening results (M6/M7).
- Anything about the real TransLoc dispatcher. It also re-optimizes (AS register), so G is not the status quo.
- That C is optimal over a night.
- **That C is affordable at high load over a full evening.** One first-hour night took 23.3 h of compute.
