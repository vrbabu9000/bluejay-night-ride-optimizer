# Proposal feedback from Prof. Kearsley, and where the midterm stands

Recorded 2 Nov 2026. Source: the instructor's written comment on the original project proposal (pasted by the team). The comment is kept verbatim below; the scorecard maps each point to the midterm package (content draft v0.4/v0.5, `docs/presentations/midterm/v0.4/`) and the design documents.

## The comment (verbatim)

> You have a strong proposal and the questions that occur to me right away are if optimization theory, as you apply it, can reduce wait times and Lyft overflow. This is a computational engineering question which, in turn, begs the questions, under what demand and fleet conditions does the rolling-horizon MILP outperform greedy assignment? I think performance gap depends on fleet size, demand intensity, or the width of the planning horizon, does it? Is it the case that the Dial-a-Ride aspect of your project make optimization particularly valuable for this service? You are free to ignore these but it is worth at least thinking about, I think. I also feel your objective/cost function needs some tuning. The phrase "...first cut Lyft overflow, then rider wait, then extra ride time, and only then van distance" a bit unclear to me. Is this a lexicographic objective function or a weighted sums with big coefficients or prefactors? I would be scared that minimizing the number/share of Lyft trips first may produce undesirable behavior if a small reduction in Lyft usage creates a very large increase in rider waiting. Finally, a few points, I would make clear Dial-a-Ride problem, I would clarify your time windows, and I would be careful using the word/phrase "exact" optimization - if you give this at an ORSA/TIMS meeting or something you will have people shouting at you, thus I would say something like "an exact MILP formulation solved repeatedly within a rolling-horizon heuristic" or something like that.

## Summary

On rigor and wording (points 4, 6, 7) we are well ahead of the proposal: the objective is explicit, the weighting is justified, and "exact" is qualified. On the question the professor found most interesting (points 2 and 3, when the MILP beats greedy), we have not moved: the project pivoted to Lyft transfer rules and the MILP-vs-greedy benchmark (M5) is still unstarted. This is likely to come up at the midterm, so the deck should name it as the planned next experiment, with the fleet / demand / cadence factors the comment suggests.
